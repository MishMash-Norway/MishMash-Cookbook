# How MishMash came about

This chapter is the centre's own record of how it was made, from the government's decision in 2023 to the opening conference in 2026. Dates are taken from public announcements and from the application archive. Where a date is approximate, the text says so.

## 2023: the AI billion

On 7 September 2023 the Norwegian government announced that research on artificial intelligence and digital technologies would be increased by at least one billion kroner over the following five years. The announcement named democracy, trust, ethics, economy, law, privacy, teaching and learning, and art and culture as central themes. It was the first time a national research investment in AI in Norway explicitly included art and culture.

## 2024: the call, and a centre for music and AI

On 8 February 2024 the Research Council of Norway announced that it would establish four to six national research centres for artificial intelligence, with a frame of 850 million kroner, and that the call would be designed during the spring. The centres were to follow three tracks: the consequences of AI for society, digital technologies and innovation, and AI for research.

The idea that became MishMash started in music. In April 2024 it was presented to the national body for music in higher education, and on 2 May 2024 a national online workshop on music and AI gathered researchers from across the country under the working title MishMash Center for Music and AI. Group work at the workshop widened the scope: the mixture of disciplines that a centre on AI and creativity needed was larger than music.

The mandatory sketch was submitted by the deadline of 7 June 2024. The Research Council received 122 sketches nationally. On 30 August 2024 a small group of the partners met in Kristiansand to plan the centre. The working name was still moving between Centre for Music, Creativity and AI, Centre for Creative AI and Centre for Creativity and AI, and the consortium had grown to eighteen partners. Feedback on the sketch arrived in September.

On 25 September 2024 a National Workshop on AI and Creativity was held at the University of Oslo and on Zoom. Participants pitched research ideas in thematic groups, discussed organisational models and mapped national and international networks. Through the autumn the partners met in an administrative workshop on 28 October and a first meeting of a MishMash council on 15 November 2024, and letters of intent were signed in December 2024 and January 2025.

## 2025: the application and the award

The application, MishMash Centre for AI and Creativity, was submitted to the Research Council by the deadline of 15 January 2025, with the University of Oslo as host and Alexander Refsum Jensenius as project leader. It proposed a national centre of sixteen higher education institutions, four research institutes and three artistic research centres, together with the National Library, the Norwegian Broadcasting Corporation, the National Museum, other museums, industry partners and international partners. Fifty applications were submitted nationally.

On 4 April 2025 the Research Council announced that 19 applications had gone to the final round, to be assessed by an international panel. MishMash was interviewed on 23 April 2025.

On 11 June 2025 the Research Council announced the six national AI centres. MishMash was one of them, alongside the Norwegian Centre for Trustworthy AI, the Artificial Intelligence Centre for the Empowerment of Human Learning, the Norwegian Centre for Embodied AI, the Norwegian Centre for Sustainable, Risk-averse and Ethical AI and the Norwegian Centre on AI for Decisions. Each centre receives up to 200 million kroner over five years. The grant letter of 20 June 2025 awarded MishMash up to 173 million kroner, subject to a revised application, which was submitted in September 2025.

The autumn of 2025 was spent building the centre. The work package leader group met in a workshop on 12 September 2025, WP2 held its kick-off on 10 October, set up the internal structures and prepared the first announcements of doctoral and postdoctoral fellowships. The first public event under the centre's name, a concert and presentation of generative music with AI and quantum computing, was held at the University of Oslo on 3 December 2025. The contract with the Research Council was signed in December, and the centre formally started on 1 December 2025.

## 2026: the launch

The administrative coordinator started in January 2026, and the first MeshUp seminar was held on 29 January. The consortium agreement between the partners was signed in March 2026, the Council was appointed on 16 March and the Board on 27 March. On 8 April 2026 the centre was formally opened at the University of Oslo, with a day of workshops, a hackathon, work package meetings and a panel on AI in creative practice, and an evening ceremony in the University Aula opened by the rector and the Minister of Research and Higher Education. Fellowships were announced at partner institutions through the spring, a Master Fellowship programme opened in August, and the opening conference was held in Kristiansand on 14 and 15 September 2026, hosted by the University of Agder in cooperation with Kilden Performing Arts Centre, Kunstsilo, Kraftverk and Cultiva. On 4 November 2026 the Minister of Research and Higher Education and the Minister of Digitalisation and Public Governance met the national AI centres at a roundtable in the government quarter, where MishMash opened on AI and copyright.
