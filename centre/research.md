# The research

MishMash studies AI from three perspectives at once: machines, meaning the systems themselves; humans, meaning creativity, identity, bodies and learning; and society, meaning law, economy, trust and culture. Across all of them the work follows three approaches. The centre creates AI systems, tools, artworks, frameworks and policies; it explores AI in creative practice, and creative methods in other domains; and it reflects on what AI does to people, culture and society. The seven work packages below are where these perspectives and approaches meet a theme. Each has a page on the website with its research questions at three reading levels, its members, projects and events.

## WP1: AI for artistic performances

The first work package asks what happens to a creative process when people and AI systems play together in real time: on stage, in interactive installations and in games. Its interest lies in systems that keep up a continuous exchange with the people using them, especially systems with several agents or with a body, such as a robot or an instrument.

Where the trend is towards very large models that are hard to steer, WP1 builds smaller systems that combine learning from data with what artists already know and with search-based methods. Performing with such a system calls for rich feedback that does not disturb the performance, so part of the work is to find out what has to be explainable when a person improvises with a machine. The tools are released as open-source software and hardware and tried out in concerts, installations and games.

The work package is led by Kyrre Glette (University of Oslo), with Ivar Grydeland (Norwegian Academy of Music) and Georgios Marentakis (Østfold University of Applied Sciences) as deputies.

Examples: the seed project [Musical Doppelgangers](https://mishmash.no/projects/musical-doppelgangers/) uses a self-playing grand piano with the Ultima festival to ask how musical gesture can be replayed without the original player, and the [WP1 Labinar](https://mishmash.no/events/wp1-labinar01/) series visits artistic labs such as MishMash Playground at the Norwegian Academy of Music. More at [mishmash.no/wp1](https://mishmash.no/wp1/).

## WP2: AI in artistic processes

The second work package looks at artists who make finished works with AI: visual art, film, virtual and extended reality, literature, performing arts, games and music. Prompt-based tools are becoming part of many workflows, and the question is what they do to the work and to the person making it.

Two concerns run through the work. One is what happens when creative decisions are handed over to a system. The other is the cultural bias built into many commercial tools, which tend to repeat dominant patterns and push other voices aside. WP2 questions the idea that a system can be neutral, examines the biases in the tools, and develops artworks and artist-centred tools in which the artist, not the machine, stays in control. Where WP1 supports live performance, WP2 works towards a finished creative product.

The work package is led by Budhaditya Chattopadhyay (University of Bergen), with Synne Tollerud Bull (Kristiania University of Applied Sciences) as deputy. The second deputy position is open for now, and will be filled soon.

Examples: [Bingo](https://mishmash.no/projects/bingo-ai-assisted-animation-documentary-film/) tests whether generative tools can be constrained to make animation for a children's documentary film without losing cultural specificity, and [AI-Assisted Film Production through Vita Vigeland](https://mishmash.no/projects/ai-assisted-film-production-vita-vigeland/) uses a screenplay as a case for AI in film pre-production. More at [mishmash.no/wp2](https://mishmash.no/wp2/).

## WP3: Creative use of AI for health and well-being

The third work package investigates how creative content made by AI, such as images, music and stories, affects people's health and well-being. It also studies AI in the creative arts therapies, and asks what AI can add to health research that draws on the arts.

Taking part in a creative process can support health, and AI tools open new ways to do so, but they also raise the worry that qualities such as empathy and autonomy lose value. Ethics is at the centre of the work. The approach is anti-oppressive and includes critical disability perspectives, and users and other stakeholders take part as partners, so that the frameworks and guidelines it develops are relevant, inclusive and fail safely.

The work package is led by Claire Ghetti (University of Bergen), with Andreas Bergsland (NTNU) and Jonna Vuoskoski (University of Oslo) as deputies.

Examples: [Creative Health Technologies](https://mishmash.no/projects/creative-health-technologies/) studies music, movement and interaction in dementia care, with the interactive sensory ball from the partner Muzziball as its case, and the [Movement-to-Music Hackathon](https://mishmash.no/projects/movement-to-music-hackathon/) at NTNU tries out markerless pose tracking for movement-to-music interaction in therapy and well-being settings. More at [mishmash.no/wp3](https://mishmash.no/wp3/).

## WP4: Creative use of AI in education

The fourth work package asks how education can use AI to open new ways of learning, foster creativity and support critical thinking and AI literacy. AI is arriving fast in classrooms and lecture halls, and with it come pedagogical, ethical and practical questions.

Schools have many local initiatives built on commercial tools, while good open teaching resources on creative AI are scarce, and hardly any exist in Norwegian. WP4 develops teaching material, models and methods with diversity, justice, inclusion, privacy and accessibility in the foreground; studies how AI is used in formal, non-formal and informal learning; and gives policy advice. It also carries findings from the other work packages into programmes and curricula.

The work package is led by Eirik Sørbø (University of Agder), with Sidsel Karlsen (Norwegian Academy of Music) and Jens Knigge (Nord University and the Norwegian Academy of Music) as deputies.

Examples: [Teacher Resources for Critical AI Literacy](https://mishmash.no/projects/teacher-resources-critical-ai-literacy/) develops professional development material with school teachers, and the [Scoping Review of Creative AI in Teaching and Learning](https://mishmash.no/projects/scoping-review-creative-ai-teaching-learning/) maps what is known so far. The work package also runs an open webinar series. More at [mishmash.no/wp4](https://mishmash.no/wp4/).

## WP5: AI in the Creative and Cultural Industries

The fifth work package studies how AI changes the way culture is produced, distributed, governed, paid for and experienced. It brings together law, the social sciences, the humanities and the arts, economics, informatics and sustainability research with the experience of cultural institutions and rights organisations.

The work is organised in four connected areas: law, rights and governance; economy, industry and labour; artistic practice and audience experience; and ethics, diversity and sustainability. Questions range from copyright and the imitation of a person's voice or face, through business models and fair distribution of value, to cultural bias and the environmental cost of AI. The aim is new knowledge, practical guidelines and sustainability assessments that can inform policy.

The work package is led by Ragnhild Brøvig (University of Oslo), with Irina Eidsvold (BI Norwegian Business School) and Jon Marius Aareskjold-Drecker (University of Tromsø and the University of Oslo) as deputies.

Examples: [Copyright and Personality Rights in the AI Era](https://mishmash.no/projects/copyright-and-personality-rights-ai-era/) analyses Nordic court decisions on the protection of performers, and [Evaluation of Cultural Bias and Workflow in AI Music Generation](https://mishmash.no/projects/evaluation-cultural-bias-workflow-ai-music-generation/) tests commercial music generators with musicians. More at [mishmash.no/wp5](https://mishmash.no/wp5/).

## WP6: AI for cultural heritage

The sixth work package investigates how AI can help discover, organise and present the collections of archives, libraries and museums. The National Library of Norway's digital collection and its Digital Humanities Lab, together with many local collections, are to be brought into one coherent framework that connects to the European Cultural Heritage Cloud.

That calls for automated curation of very large collections. One focus is music, with hybrid models that combine machine learning with musicology and with symbolic AI grounded in how people hear. Particular attention goes to minority cultural expressions, including Norwegian folk music and Sámi joik. The work package holds that public institutions must lead in these technologies, and it develops policies and guidelines so that heritage is used ethically, with proper citation and without plagiarism.

The work package is led by Ingrid Romarheim Haugen (National Library of Norway), with Arnulf Mattes (University of Bergen) and Olivier Lartillot (University of Oslo) as deputies.

Examples: [AI-Assisted Curation and Transcription of Norwegian Folk Music](https://mishmash.no/projects/ai-assisted-curation-and-transcription-of-norwegian-folk-music/), a collaboration between the National Library and the University of Oslo, segments and transcribes the library's folk music recordings, and [Archival Intelligence](https://mishmash.no/projects/archival-intelligence/) is an exhibition and seminar on archives and the datasets used to train AI. More at [mishmash.no/wp6](https://mishmash.no/wp6/).

## WP7: Human-centric AI for Creative Problem-Solving

The seventh work package takes creative AI outside the arts. It asks how a person or a group can use creative AI to solve a specific task, from industrial design to emergency rescue, where a pressing problem has to be solved with the resources at hand.

The people it works with, such as designers, film makers, industrial operators and emergency personnel, need freedom and surprise from the system, but also control: they must be able to judge, keep and discard what the AI suggests. The systems work within stricter limits than elsewhere in the centre, from the laws of physics to legal and safety requirements, and WP7 builds those limits into the principles the systems are designed on. A central question is the tension between creative freedom and what the job requires.

The work package is led by Carsten Griwodz (University of Oslo), with Baltasar Beferull-Lozano (SimulaMet) and Sashi Komandur (University of Inland Norway) as deputies.

Examples: [Strategic Storyteller](https://mishmash.no/projects/strategic-storyteller/) compares a tailored storytelling system with general language models, and [When Creativity Meets Criticality](https://mishmash.no/projects/when-creativity-meets-criticality/) is a workshop on creative AI for decision-making in crises. More at [mishmash.no/wp7](https://mishmash.no/wp7/).
