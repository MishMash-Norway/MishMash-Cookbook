# What MishMash is

MishMash is a Norwegian research centre on artificial intelligence and creativity, one of six national AI centres funded by the Research Council of Norway from the government's AI billion. The full name is MishMash Centre for AI and Creativity. The centre is hosted by the University of Oslo and gathers more than 200 researchers and practitioners from universities, university colleges, research institutes, artistic research centres, cultural institutions and companies across Norway, with international partners.

## Why the name

A mishmash is a mixture of things that were never meant to go together. The application opened by noting the confusing mixture of notions and opinions about AI's potential and risks in academia, the creative industries, cultural institutions and society. The centre keeps the word as its name because the mixture is the point: computer scientists and choreographers, lawyers and game designers, museum curators and music therapists, in the same room at the same time.

## What the centre does

MishMash studies AI from the perspective of the fundamental human trait of creativity, understood as the ability to form novel and meaningful ideas or works. Its primary objective, in the words of the application, is to create, explore and reflect on AI for, through and in creative practices. The overarching research question asks what the possibilities, limitations and transformative effects of AI on creative practices are, and how co-creative AI systems can be developed that complement human creativity while addressing ethical, cultural and societal implications.

The application set out seven challenges, one for each work package.

1. Real-time AI systems for immersive, dynamic and ethical human-machine collaboration in artistic performance.
2. AI in artists' creative processes, with control, and with attention to bias, cultural implications and environmental impact.
3. AI-generated content and creative AI in health, well-being and therapeutic practice, with empathy, consent and equity.
4. Creative AI in education, for learning and AI literacy, with diversity, justice, inclusion and well-being.
5. AI for innovation in the creative and cultural industries, with copyright, rights management, sustainability and fair revenue distribution.
6. AI for the preservation, accessibility and representation of cultural heritage in archives, libraries and museums, within ethical and legal limits.
7. Creative AI for human agency, control and expression in problem-solving under physical, legal and societal constraints.

## How the centre works

The centre is a mostly virtual research environment. It runs weekly online MeshUp seminars, work package meetings, thematic webinars, public workshops, seed funding for small joint projects across partners, a Master Fellowship programme for students at partner institutions, and one large conference each semester. Results are registered in the national research information system NVA and listed automatically on the website.
