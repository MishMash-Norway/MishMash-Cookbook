# Values and open research

A centre that studies the ethics of AI has to be able to answer for its own conduct. MishMash has written down how its members treat each other, how it shares what it produces, how it uses AI itself and which languages it speaks. This chapter summarises those commitments; the full texts are on the website.

## Code of conduct

MishMash brings together people from many artistic and scientific disciplines and from private and public institutions, and the mix only works if everyone can take part on equal terms. The [code of conduct](https://mishmash.no/about/code-of-conduct/) applies to all the centre's activities.

It names six values. Curiosity: members are there to learn from one another. Generosity: they help newcomers into the network and share knowledge, methods, data and code. Care: they remember that people take part from different institutions, career stages and life situations. Integrity: they hold high ethical standards and are open about how results came about. Accountability: they consider the biases and the environmental and social costs of the AI systems they use and study. Cultural heritage: they include traditions beyond the dominant ones, and use a work only as its makers or performers, or their families, have agreed.

It also sets out how people work together. Members meet one another as colleagues and assume that someone who disagrees has reasons not yet heard. They respect working hours and holidays, give every meeting a clear purpose, and choose formats with the environmental footprint in mind. They agree on authorship early, name everyone who contributed, say what AI tools contributed, and use people's material only as agreed.

Harassment has no place in the centre. Anyone who experiences or sees a breach can speak to the organiser of the activity, to the management, or write to contact@mishmash.no, also on behalf of someone else. Because MishMash is not a legal entity and has no employer responsibility, a case that needs a formal process follows the rules of the institution where the people involved work or study, and the management helps find the right channel.

## Open research

MishMash works in the open by default, and it says open research rather than open science on purpose. Artistic research produces performances, exhibitions, scores, instruments and documentation, not only articles and datasets, and a term that covers only science would leave much of the centre's work, and the people who make it, outside. The commitments are set out on the [open research](https://mishmash.no/about/open-research/) page.

Publications go to open access channels, often with an early version in a preprint archive. The centre encourages co-authorship across disciplines and institutions, and sees the amount of co-publishing as a measure of how well the interdisciplinary dialogue works.

Research data should follow the FAIR principles: findable, accessible, interoperable and reusable. Data are shared as open as possible and as closed as necessary, given privacy and copyright, in standard open formats, with documentation and a data management plan. Where the centre can choose, data carry a permissive licence, normally CC BY. The deposit routes are described in [Tools, data and compute](infrastructure.md).

Software and hardware developed in the centre are released as open source under permissive licences. Which licences is still under discussion, since a freelancer, an institution and a company have different interests.

Open research is a requirement from the Research Council of Norway, but the centre treats it as more than that. Permissive licences make tools available to people and institutions that cannot buy them, in Norway and abroad, and the centre counts this as part of working responsibly with AI. The website follows the same line: its code is MIT-licensed, its text is CC BY 4.0 and the data files it makes itself are CC0, as set out in the [terms of use](https://mishmash.no/about/terms/).

## The AI colophon

MishMash studies creative uses of AI and uses AI openly in its own communication. The [AI colophon](https://mishmash.no/about/ai-colophon/) declares where. AI assistance is used to draft and adapt texts, including the three reading levels of many pages and the glossary entries, which human editors review and revise. Much of the site's code and automation is written with AI assistance and marked as such in the public commit history. Machine translation is used between English and Norwegian and marked on the pages concerned.

Three commitments follow. Humans are responsible: AI output is reviewed before it goes live, except the nightly updates from NVA and ORCID, and errors belong to the centre, not the machines. Nothing is hidden: AI involvement is declared, marked and traceable. And the centre's own use of AI is part of its research, a way to create, explore and reflect on the tools it studies.

The colophon also reports the weight of the site: each build estimates the carbon per page visit.

## Languages

The website is written in English, with the main pages mirrored in Norwegian in both written forms. The Bokmål pages are written by hand. The Nynorsk pages are generated from them at build time with Apertium, an open-source rule-based translator, and the centre's own terms and the national termbases are checked in the output. The [languages](https://mishmash.no/about/languages/) page explains the method and counts how much text there is in each language.

The Nynorsk edition is an open experiment. Its pages are not proofread before publication, and each says so, with a link to the Bokmål original, until a member who writes Nynorsk has corrected it. So that unreviewed machine Nynorsk does not end up training future language models, the generated pages are reserved against text and data mining and closed to the crawlers that collect training data.

The Sámi languages and Kven are not machine translated on the site. No translator of publishable quality exists for them, and the people who build Sámi language technology advise against publishing unedited output. A core set of pages in North Sámi and in Kven, translated by people, is planned with the partner institutions responsible for those languages.
