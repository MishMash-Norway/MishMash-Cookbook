# How MishMash is organised

MishMash is hosted by the University of Oslo and led by a director, two deputy directors, a research adviser and an administrative coordinator. The centre started on paper on 1 December 2025 and counts 2026 as its first year and 2030 as its fifth.

## Bodies

The centre has five bodies, described on the website under [Organisation](https://mishmash.no/about/organisation/).

- The **Management group** handles day-to-day coordination, administration, budget and communication.
- The **Work Package Leader Group** consists of the leader and two "sidekicks" of each of the seven work packages, and coordinates the research across them.
- The **Board** governs the centre and takes the strategic decisions. It was appointed on 27 March 2026 and is led by the rector of the University of Agder.
- The **Council** has one member from each consortium partner. It was appointed on 16 March 2026, after the consortium agreement was signed, and appoints the Board.
- The **Scientific Advisory Board** consists of twelve international experts in law, creative computing, film, music technology, education, arts and media research.

A Stakeholder Board for associated partners and the cultural and creative sectors is planned.

## Seven work packages

The research is organised in seven work packages, each led by a group of three people from different institutions.

| Work package | Theme |
| --- | --- |
| WP1 | AI for artistic performances |
| WP2 | AI in artistic processes |
| WP3 | Creative use of AI for health and well-being |
| WP4 | Creative use of AI in education |
| WP5 | AI in the Creative and Cultural Industries |
| WP6 | AI for cultural heritage |
| WP7 | Human-centric AI for Creative Problem-Solving |

Work packages 1 and 2 address AI in artistic performance and production, 3 to 6 address creative uses of AI in health, education, the creative and cultural industries and cultural heritage, and 7 addresses creative AI for problem-solving outside the arts.

## Three approaches

Across the work packages, the work follows three approaches that the centre uses as a motto.

- **Create** AI systems, tools, artworks, frameworks and policies.
- **Explore** AI in creative practice, and creative methods in other domains.
- **Reflect** on what AI does to people, culture and society.

## Three perspectives

The application organised the centre's "mishmash" into a mesh, drawn as a cube, where the seven work packages cross three perspectives: machines (the systems themselves), humans (creativity, identity, bodies and learning) and society (law, economy, trust and culture). The three perspectives are used for cross-work-package meetings and for the plenary sessions of the centre's conferences.
