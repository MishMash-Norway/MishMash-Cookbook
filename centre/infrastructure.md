# Tools, data and compute

Research on creative AI needs machines, places to keep data and rooms to try things out. This chapter describes what the centre offers its members and where it points them. The website's [FAQ](https://mishmash.no/faq/) gives the details.

## Computing

The first question is which of three jobs is at hand. Running a model that someone else trained, called inference, is most of what people do, and a good laptop or a single consumer GPU often suffices. Adapting a model to one's own recordings, footage or drawings, called fine-tuning, needs a workstation GPU or some hours on a cluster, and this is where most creative research happens. Training a model from scratch needs a national or European supercomputer, and few projects should do it.

The advice is to move up one step at a time: one's own machine, the department's machine, the institution's cluster, the national systems and finally Europe. Most creative work never needs to go past the second step. Work that has to respond in real time on stage belongs on a machine in the room, not in a data centre.

MishMash has projects of its own on Educloud Research at the University of Oslo and on the national systems run by Sigma2, and members can be added to them whatever their institution. Educloud offers desktops, shared storage, notebooks in the browser and the Fox cluster with GPU nodes, and is approved for confidential data. Sigma2 provides the national computing and storage systems for work too large for a workstation or a local cluster. Members at partners outside Norway can be added if they can log in through ID-porten with their national electronic ID. The centre is also arranging for an IT specialist to help researchers across the consortium find the right resources.

The large partner universities run their own chat gateways, transcription services, clusters such as Fox at the University of Oslo and Idun at NTNU, and secure environments for confidential data. Institutions without a cluster are still eligible for the national systems, since eligibility follows the type of institution, not its hardware. Creative Computing Hub Oslo, at the University of Oslo, is a low-threshold platform for machine learning in creative practice that is also open to freelancers.

Beyond Norway, publicly funded research can use EuroHPC AI for Science and the LUMI AI Factory free of charge, and small and medium enterprises have their own free routes. Partners outside Norway are pointed to their own country's national provider.

The website is frank about the gaps. No Norwegian institutional AI service runs models for image, sound, music or video; the gateways handle text and speech only. For these media the route is open-weight models on hardware the researcher controls. Artists without an employer, freelancers and most cultural institutions have the least access to the tools they most need, and closing that gap is one of the reasons the centre exists.

## Data

Datasets are deposited in trusted repositories with a persistent identifier, such as [DataverseNO](https://dataverse.no/) or the centre's [Zenodo community](https://zenodo.org/communities/mishmash/), and registered in NVA, from which the website's [results](https://mishmash.no/results/) page is generated. Artistic material belongs in repositories made for creative practice, such as [Freesound](https://freesound.org/) and the [Research Catalogue](https://www.researchcatalogue.net/). Material from libraries and museums connects to the European cultural heritage infrastructure. The principles behind these routes are set out in [Values and open research](values.md).

The website publishes its own data, on events, institutions, people and projects, as open files at [mishmash.no/data](https://mishmash.no/data/).

## Labs and studios

Much of the work happens in the partners' own labs, studios and collections rather than in central facilities. Several partners run their own clusters and GPU workstations, which suit real-time work best. Artistic labs open their doors to the network: the MishMash Playground at the Norwegian Academy of Music is a monthly workshop series for artists working with new AI technology. The National Library of Norway brings its large digital collection and its Digital Humanities Lab to the work on cultural heritage.

## The website as an experiment

The website at mishmash.no is itself one of the centre's projects. It is a static site built from a [public repository on GitHub](https://github.com/MishMash-Norway/mishmash-web), where anyone in the network can read the source and propose a change. Every change is checked for broken links, valid HTML and accessibility before it is published, and profiles and results are refreshed every night from NVA and ORCID.

Many pages are written at three reading levels, so that a school class and a fellow researcher can read the same page. Machine-generated Nynorsk and the declared use of AI are described in [Values and open research](values.md). The site also has a [lab](https://mishmash.no/lab/) of browser experiments and alternative interfaces built by students. The project behind it all is described on the [website project page](https://mishmash.no/projects/the-mishmash-website/).
